PIPPA

Purity Interpretation and Processing for Pre-sequencing Assessment

How to use PIPPA
PIPPA classifies each DNA sample as Use as-is, Borderline, or Must cleanup or repeat based on NanoDrop spectrophotometry data and optional fluorescence quantification. It applies configurable quality thresholds that shift automatically with your chosen conservatism and downstream-tolerance settings.

What you need

  • A NanoDrop export in .xlsx or .xls format
  • Columns for concentration (ng/µL), A260/A280, and A260/A230 — any subset works
  • Optional: a Qubit or PicoGreen fluorescence concentration column

Column naming

PIPPA auto-detects columns by matching common header names. These patterns always work:

  • Concentration: anything containing ng/µL, nanodrop, or nucleic acid
  • A260/A280: anything containing 260/280
  • A260/A230: anything containing 260/230
  • Fluorescence: anything containing Qubit, PicoGreen, or fluor

If auto-detection is wrong, use the dropdowns in Step 3 to correct the mapping.

The three decisions

  • Use as-is — all quality metrics are within acceptable limits; proceed to library prep.
  • Borderline — one or more metrics are marginal; consider the downstream application before proceeding.
  • Must cleanup or repeat — at least one critical metric is outside acceptable limits; cleanup or re-extraction is required.

Presets explained

  • Research (Permissive) — relaxed thresholds for exploratory work where sample scarcity outweighs purity concerns.
  • Standard — balanced defaults suitable for most NGS library preparations.
  • Service Lab (Strict) — tightened thresholds for fee-for-service labs where downstream failures carry financial or reputational risk.
